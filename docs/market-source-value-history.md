# Public card value history

The shipped sports inventory carries 5,366 verified public claim records, identified by collectible ID and claim hash. These cover 5,364 distinct collectible IDs, across 2,577 sport/card/rarity groups. All 5,366 records carry their verified opening date, canonical birth value and available current public owner names. Claim records and distinct collectible IDs are different counts.

Historical pricing is supported by the native public first-paint proof at `app/lib/game/sportsEconomyFirstPaintProof.v1.json`. Its source-carried date is **2026-06-23T20:01:03.454Z**. SHA-256 of `JSON.stringify(proof.payload)` independently matches **85120bb4173456c0f19bcc98189f76b220fe682f52b53d6ef0283f905b11a2db**. This digest identifies the public pricing payload; it grants no ownership or transaction authority.

That record reports **$426,947,017.33** across 1,962 groups: 1,197 single-priced-copy observations, 745 group totals and 20 groups without a supplied current price. Single-copy observations retain their exact carried claim identity. Group totals remain aggregate and are counted once; they are never divided into invented individual prices. The group count and total must agree with the carried row observations before snapshot coverage is admitted.

`historyObservation` carries the source date, amount, scope, priced/vault counts, payload hash, and the existing public model operands retained at that record. Single-copy records must match a carried collectible ID and claim hash; their model seed must be that same claim. Aggregate records carry exact sport/card/rarity group identity matching the target row, and their model representative must be the row's claim or a known claim in that group. Representative changes can retain history only through exact carried membership. The two historical group representatives no longer present among current birth records remain group-scoped under their exact original source row identities. `sportsHistorySnapshot` carries the complete recorded scope and total. `sourceEvaluation` separately preserves the later reported model coordinate and amount. An evaluation date does not create card issuance or a new performance event. A genuinely newer pricing capture replaces the current operands only from its supplied capture/evaluation coordinate onward.

Each claim enters history at its own verified seal with its canonical birth value. For example, J. Misiorowski opens on **2026-05-10T23:46:38.259Z** with a birth value of **$87,171,058**; its first whole presentation pulse is 12,057,380. Its June 23 recorded value is **$355,860,612.49**. The available source does not establish the intervening performance-price event dates, so history holds the birth value until the actual dated observation. It does not backdate the June valuation to opening or assert that all performance changes occurred on June 23.

The former October 14 coordinate, pulse 14,647,306, is a later evaluation of retained June inputs. There are no card openings at that pulse. Its original reported total, **$437,773,300.9889**, remains provenance metadata rather than an artificial $289 million historical catch-up.

The 615 discovery groups absent from the original price catalog contain 698 claim records with no supplied individual current quote. Their exact births remain available. Later pricing coverage is explicitly bounded by its capture, so later seals never appear before opening and unavailable current quotes are never represented as individual zero-dollar valuations. Complete dated per-copy valuation models supersede older aggregate coverage. Current totals continue to use the original pricing operands and formula.

The shipped chart uses step interpolation and policy `sealed-births-and-source-captures-v3`, which invalidates earlier backcast caches.

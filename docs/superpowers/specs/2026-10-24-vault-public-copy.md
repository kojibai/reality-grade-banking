## The file can travel. The key stays yours.

Save your encrypted Vault somewhere you trust. A copy of that file alone cannot unlock the money inside. Opening it requires your authorized passkey and your device's verification.

Your Vault belongs to your existing identity. One account. An extra layer of protection where you choose it.

## Three simple steps

### 1. Give it a place.

Create a Vault and give it a name. Add money from your balance or card, or choose a supported asset to put inside. The asset stays itself, with its ownership and original proof.

### 2. Make it yours.

Authorize with your passkey. Your device may use Face ID, a fingerprint or its PIN. Save and reopen your encrypted recovery file before money moves. Then approve funding and save the updated Vault.

### 3. Open it when you need it.

Open your saved Vault. Add more, return value to your balance or send directly to another Receiz ID. Review the destination and what is leaving, then authorize. The original record stays connected to what happened.

## Show what you hold. Keep control of it.

Sometimes you need proof, not a transfer.

Choose **Share proof** to create a separate record of the holdings you want to show. The other person can open it, verify its evidence and read its details with compatible software. They receive proof of the disclosed ownership at that recorded moment.

They do not receive your unlock key, account access or permission to spend. Sharing the proof leaves your value in your Vault.

## Your original goes with you.

The private Vault file carries its contained originals, their history and your encrypted recovery material. A compatible independent reader can open and verify them. The Vault screen is one way to use the object; the object keeps the record.

**Save Vault** keeps your private original. **Share proof** creates a separate, deliberately limited record for someone else.

## Less exposed. More in your control.

**A copied file isn't an open door.**

The saved Vault is encrypted. Someone who gets the file still needs its authorized passkey to unlock it.

**Your passkey knows where it belongs.**

A lookalike website cannot request a passkey registered for the real site's domain. Check the address before you act.

**A record you can follow.**

Your value keeps its original evidence and history. You can inspect what entered the Vault, what you sent and what remains in its recorded history.

**The same money can't be returned twice.**

The original funded value is consumed once. Reopening a consumed copy does not create more money.

## Protect what matters to you.

The reserve you don't want mixed into everyday spending. The money set aside for a major purchase. An owned asset you want behind an additional key. The proof of ownership you need to show without handing over control.

You decide what goes inside. You approve what comes out.

## A new phone shouldn't mean starting over.

Keep your encrypted Vault file and access to its authorized passkey. On a supported replacement device, use both to open the same Vault and recover its original record.

Check that recovery works when you set it up. Your Identity Record alone cannot replace a missing Vault file or passkey.

## Straight answers

**Is the money still in my everyday balance?**

No. Depositing value moves its custody into the Vault so it cannot also be spent from your available balance or holdings. Returning it uses the same money and ownership rules.

**Does adding an asset sell it?**

No. A supported asset moves into the Vault as that asset. Its ownership, quantity, rights, backing and original proof stay connected.

**Can someone spend with the proof I share?**

No. Share proof is a separate public record with no private key or spending authority. It shows the selected holdings and the evidence behind them at the recorded moment.

**Can someone unlock it with a screenshot of my face?**

Vault uses your device's passkey verification. A picture of your face is not the passkey or the private encryption key.

**Can I open it without internet?**

You can open already saved encrypted contents when the required passkey is available locally. An offline file shows its saved record. Connect to check changes made on another device, fund a Vault or return its money.

**Does this prevent every kind of fraud?**

It protects against specific risks, including someone obtaining only your encrypted Vault file. It does not make an unsafe deal safe or protect a device someone fully controls. Keep your device and passkey account protected, and approve only actions you understand.

**Can I use my usual account?**

Yes. Vault lives inside Identity. Your existing account stays yours.

## Claim-to-evidence ledger (not public copy)

- Copied file cannot unlock: authenticated encryption plus a PRF-derived key not included in the export; adversarial unlock tests.
- Domain-aware passkey: validated RP/origin binding; https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3/#relying-party-identifier
- Device verification: authenticator authorization, not website face recognition; https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3/#user-verification
- Source continuity: native funded Note originals and verified credit source preserved in the encrypted journal.
- Once-only return: same exact native allocation/consumption rules, tested under retries and copied files; not a claim that encryption itself prevents double spending.
- Replacement-device recovery: saved encrypted file and the same PRF-capable credential; must be exercised on supported real devices before publication.
- Private original portability: exact admitted contained sources and continuity, protected custody material and independent format/reader; an account summary or external asset URL is insufficient.
- Public ownership proof: selected admitted source witnesses and source-bound holder signature; no private source/custody material or login/spending capability; historical scope, not an assertion of permanent unspent status.
- Asset deposits: native ownership transfer and separately qualified Market-share certificate transfer. Unsupported asset classes cannot be included by copying their image or recording a claimed valuation.

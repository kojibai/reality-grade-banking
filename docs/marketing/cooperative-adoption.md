# Cooperative economy: from one existing relationship to a qualified exchange

This is a separate cooperative recruitment suite. The $149/month Business offer and all 30 Business audience pages remain their own funnel. Cooperative assessment pricing is not invented on this page.

## Start with the buyer who already has the problem

Prioritize established producer, wholesale/distribution and manufacturing cooperatives with repeat commercial counterparties. Speak with the general manager, finance lead or operations lead who can describe one order, one supplier obligation and the payment gap. Then federations with participating businesses ready to act; worker, energy and housing organizations each have their own page and operating requirements. This prioritization is an adoption hypothesis to measure, not a claim of existing clients.

The useful first question is: **What do your members already have that another business needs—and what is holding up the next exchange?**

Use their terms: stock, supplier payments, member returns, delivery records, board approval, who can sign, time spent reconstructing the handover. Introduce proof objects by showing the source and defined right that move. Keep Settlement, Reserve and asset rights distinct.

## Audience-language evidence

External research informs operator vocabulary only; repository law and implementations determine product truth. CoBank emphasizes collections, cash positions, working capital and disbursement control in its [cash management material](https://www.cobank.com/services/cash-management). NCBA CLUSA identifies cooperative access to capital and compatibility with member ownership as concerns in its [SSBCI comment](https://ncbaclusa.coop/content/uploads/2021/08/NCBA-CLUSA-comment-on-SSBCI-6.4.2021.pdf). These support the cash-timing/member-authority language; they do not establish any Receiz functionality or guaranteed outcome. Research checked 2026-10-24.

## A repeatable daily process

1. Identify five organizations with public evidence of active production and repeat trading relationships. Record source URL, operating fit, likely decision maker and one specific question. Prefer fewer relevant organizations to bulk lists.
2. Prepare three short, individually relevant introductions. Example: “When a buyer takes time to pay, which next purchase gets held up? This shows how one existing cooperative exchange can keep its source and ownership record connected. Would one of your current supplier relationships be a useful starting point?” Use the matching sector URL. Sending outreach requires the owner’s authorization; this document does not send anything.
3. Review existing opted-in native inquiries and appointments. Follow up on their actual question. Carry their context into the assessment rather than asking them to start over.
4. For each qualified conversation, identify the source asset/right, two willing counterparties, authorized signers, current records, commercial terms and applicable receiving path. Decide the next concrete work item, responsible person and review date.
5. Track relevant replies, completed conversations, booked assessments, qualified first exchanges, approved commercial scopes and measured outcomes. A submission or appointment is not revenue. The source native conversation and calendar remain authoritative; a sales worksheet does not replace them.

## The assessment and commercial next step

Map existing value → existing counterparties → friction → implemented capability → source/admission/qualification → one agreed exchange → measured result. Capture organization, region, members/business count, goods/services, relevant relationships, bottleneck and desired route naturally through the conversation; monthly activity is optional. The Twin uses its configured native memory and permissions. No public form should request private signing keys, Identity Records or production credentials.

An assessment must result in a scoped integration proposal or an explicit missing requirement. Describe deliverables, responsibilities, evidence of acceptance and commercial terms before any payment request. Do not market the assessment as funding development of infrastructure that already exists.
